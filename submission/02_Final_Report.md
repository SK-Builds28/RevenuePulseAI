# RevenuePulse AI - Final Report

**Sales Opportunity & Customer Revenue-Decline Prediction Copilot**
MBA ZG583 capstone · Individual submission · Suhani Kanwar · Phase 3 MVP

> **Evidence boundary.** Every model number in this report comes from synthetic
> account data generated by this repository. Synthetic evaluation does not
> establish real-world model performance, willingness to pay, retention impact or
> commercial traction.

---

## 1. Problem and opportunity

A B2B account team's early-warning workflow is manual. Monthly revenue, order
frequency, product adoption, engagement, support friction and activity recency sit
in different systems, at different grains, with different refresh cycles. At
quarter end a manager must decide which of 150-400 accounts deserve attention, using
a spreadsheet or a dashboard that shows history without ranking it.

Three things break:

1. **Coverage.** No manager can inspect the whole book, so review effort follows
   recency and familiarity rather than risk.
2. **Timing.** A rule that looks for revenue that already fell identifies accounts
   after the intervention window has narrowed.
3. **Consistency.** Two managers with the same book would produce different
   priorities, and neither could explain the ordering.

The opportunity is not "another dashboard". It is a defensible **ordering** of the
book against a defined future outcome, with evidence a manager can check in under a
minute, produced inside the review capacity they already have.

## 2. Target users

| User | Job in the workflow | What they need from the product |
|---|---|---|
| Sales manager | Owns the book; allocates review time | A short, ranked queue with clear high-risk flags |
| Key account manager | Runs the relationship | Evidence and a starting point for a conversation |
| RevOps / revenue strategy analyst | Owns data quality, definitions, reporting | A reproducible definition of risk, monitoring and audit trail |

Secondary stakeholders: finance (materiality definition), customer success (health
signals), and governance/privacy reviewers before any real-data pilot.

## 3. Product vision

A quarterly review ritual in which every account has a defensible risk estimate, the
manager sees the 50 that matter most, each one carries checkable evidence and a
suggested next step, and every human decision is captured so the ranking can be
improved and audited.

Long term, RevenuePulse becomes the prioritisation layer above CRM and billing data:
it does not replace either system and it never acts on its own. It answers "who
first, and why" and leaves "what exactly to do" with the human.

## 4. Jobs to be done

- **Functional:** "When the quarter-end review starts, help me decide which accounts
  to review first, so I do not waste the review window on accounts that are fine."
- **Functional:** "When I open an account, show me the evidence behind its position
  so I can defend the decision to my manager."
- **Emotional:** "When I skip an account, help me feel that the omission was a
  conscious trade-off, not an oversight."
- **Social:** "When I raise an account in a review meeting, give me a shared,
  explainable basis rather than a personal hunch."
- **Anti-job:** "Do not contact my customer, and do not change anything commercial
  without me."

## 5. Why AI instead of a dashboard or rules alone

| Approach | What it gives | Why it is not enough alone |
|---|---|---|
| Static dashboard | History, filters, charts | No ordering against a future outcome; review burden unchanged |
| Hand-written rule | Transparent, cheap, auditable | Only encodes what an analyst already believes; misses interactions; on this data it was near random |
| Learned model | Combined signal, calibrated ordering, capacity-aware evaluation | Needs data discipline, monitoring and explanation; can learn artefacts |
| LLM narrative | Fluent summaries | No ranking guarantee, hallucination risk, cost, and no evaluation basis here |

The decisive argument is capacity. A manager can review 50 of 370 accounts. The
question is which 50. On the locked synthetic test split, the transparent scorecard
found 7 decliners in 50; the learned model found 27. That is a prioritisation
difference, not an accuracy difference, and it is measured at the capacity the
product assumes.

The honest counter-argument, and the reason this is not a "scale" recommendation:
the baseline is weak, the data is synthetic, and a better rule might close much of
the gap.

## 6. Opportunity framing

Framed bottom-up as hypotheses to test, not as market sizing:

- **Wedge:** mid-market B2B account teams with 150-400 accounts, quarterly reviews,
  and account-level monthly aggregates already available in billing/CRM exports.
- **Value pool:** review hours spent scanning accounts, plus revenue decline that is
  identified too late to intervene.
- **Adoption path:** replace a spreadsheet filter with a ranked queue inside an
  existing ritual. No new system of record, no new data collection.
- **Constraints:** aggregate-only data access, privacy review, no automated action,
  and a client able to name a materiality threshold for "decline".

No market size, growth rate, competitor count or willingness-to-pay figure is
claimed. Those require research that was not performed.

## 7. Build vs buy vs API

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Build (chosen)** | scikit-learn pipeline, Streamlit prototype, local SQLite audit log. Fits the constraint that the core prediction must work locally, be inspectable by faculty, and add no LLM dependency. |
| **Buy** (CRM-native scoring, CS platforms) | Faster to deploy but black-box scoring, seat-based pricing, and no control over the label definition - which is the core intellectual work here. |
| **API** (hosted AutoML / LLM) | Would add vendor dependency, data-egress questions and cost per call for a problem that a 22-feature tabular model already handles deterministically. Rejected for the MVP; possible later for orchestration, not for the core score. |

The MVP is deliberately boring infrastructure: one artifact, one CSV contract, one
SQLite file, no network calls at runtime.

## 8. First wedge

One segment (mid-market), one quarter-end cadence, one review cohort (50 accounts),
one definition of material decline agreed with finance, and a human decision on every
row. Success is judged against the client's current rule on the same review windows,
not against a generic benchmark.

## 9. PRD summary

**Goal:** let a sales manager identify the accounts most likely to show material
revenue decline in the next 90 days and review them in priority order.

**In scope (MVP):** ranked queue for a selected quarter-end; risk bands from
configured probability thresholds; reason codes; rule-based next-best-action with
evidence; accept / edit / dismiss with note; local audit log; CSV export of queue and
log; visible synthetic-data and "not a churn prediction" disclaimers.

**Out of scope:** automated customer contact, offers, pricing changes, email
generation, CRM write-back, LLM text, authentication, multi-tenancy, multi-user
roles, opportunity/expansion scoring as a primary output.

**Non-goals:** proving real-world accuracy, replacing the CRM, or making commercial
decisions.

## 13. MVP scope

Four pages, one reviewer, one date at a time, aggregate data only, local audit log,
no auth. The MVP exists to test the workflow claim: can a manager go from "open
tool" to "defensible decision" in under a minute per account, with the AI assisting
rather than deciding?

## 14. User journey

1. Manager opens the dashboard, sees 370 accounts, 6 high, 29 medium, 335 low for
   2025-09-30, and a synthetic-data warning.
2. Opens the Review Queue, filters to Enterprise plus high/medium, sets top 25.
3. Scans: risk probability, band, recent revenue, trend, engagement, days since
   activity, primary reason, status.
4. Opens the highest account: sees probability 0.87, the revenue history, the
   signals table and reason codes ("recent revenue decline; revenue well above its
   prior six-month average; reduced transaction frequency").
5. Reads the suggested action and its evidence; judges that a health review is
   justified and notes the specific question to ask.
6. Records **Edit** with a sharper action, or **Accept**, or **Dismiss** with a
   reason ("known seasonal billing").
7. The decision is stored locally; the queue shows the account as accepted/edited/
   dismissed; the log exports to CSV for the review meeting.
8. No customer is contacted by the system, at any point.

## 15. Human-in-the-loop design

- The AI output is a **suggestion plus evidence**, never an executed action.
- Every account decision requires an explicit accept / edit / dismiss.
- Editing is first-class: the reviewer can replace the suggested action, and both
  versions are stored.
- Dismissal requires no justification in the MVP, but a note field is encouraged and
  stored when provided.
- Review status is visible in the queue, so an unreviewed high-risk account is
  obvious.
- The product is designed to make disagreement cheap; the audit log records it.
- Known failure mode: automation bias. Mitigation is procedural (require a note on
  high-risk dismissals in a pilot), not solved by the MVP.

## 16. Trust and explainability

- **Model card** with intended use, performance, limitations and prohibited uses.
- **Reason codes** are rules over the account's own features, so a claim can always
  be checked against the row.
- **Calibration** is reported (Brier 0.094; mean bin gap 0.030) rather than assumed.
- **Uncertainty** is stated with account-level bootstrap intervals, not point
  estimates alone.
- **Wording discipline:** "factors associated with the risk score include ...";
  never "because", "caused by", "will churn".
- **The counter-intuitive driver is disclosed** in the model card: the model relies
  on a positive six-month revenue trend, which is a synthetic-data property.
- **No fabricated explanations:** no LLM is used to generate text about an account.

## 17. Fallback logic

| Failure | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Snapshot data missing | Page shows the missing path and the exact rebuild command; no score is shown |
| Model artifact missing or corrupt | Controlled error with retrain instruction; queue not rendered |
| Required feature columns missing | Data error listing the columns; scoring refuses rather than guessing |
| Row with all core level fields missing | Row excluded from the queue; the count of excluded rows is shown |
| Medium risk | Presented as "Review recommended", not definitive risk |
| Low risk | Presented as "Monitor - no immediate action" |
| Review DB missing | Treated as an empty log; first decision creates it |
| No review dates available | Sidebar error; app stops instead of showing an empty queue |

## 18. Data design

- **Grain:** one row per account per calendar month; snapshots at quarter end.
- **Fields (synthetic):** `account_id`, `month`, `segment`, `monthly_revenue`,
  `transaction_count`, `engagement_score`, `days_since_last_activity`,
  `product_count`, `support_interactions`, `contract_age_months`, `is_synthetic`.
- **No personal data:** no names, emails, phone numbers, addresses or real company
  identifiers. `account_id` is a synthetic sequence and is never a model feature.
- **Point-in-time discipline:** features use only months on or before the snapshot
  date; the label uses only the following three months.
- **Feature allow-list:** 22 features in `src/features/snapshots.py`; identifiers,
  split metadata, target-period fields and `is_synthetic` are excluded by
  construction and tested.
- **Missing values:** engagement (2.9%) and activity recency (2.2%) missing at
  source; short-history features are null by design and imputed inside the pipeline
  with train statistics.
- **Pilot data contract:** account IDs plus monthly aggregates only, encrypted
  transfer, deletion after 30 days, no CRM write access required.

## 19. Label definition

For a quarter-end snapshot `t`:

```text
reference = mean monthly revenue over the 3 months ending at t
future    = mean monthly revenue over the 3 months after t
label     = 1 if future <= reference * (1 - 20/100), else 0
```

Eligibility: at least 6 months of history, a complete 3-month future window, and
`reference >= 5,000` synthetic currency units.

This is a **revenue-decline proxy**, not confirmed churn. It cannot distinguish a
retention problem from seasonality, billing timing or a product-mix shift. The
definition was fixed in Phase 1 and was not changed in Phase 2 to improve results.
In the synthetic dataset, prevalence is 12.8% (503 / 3,921 snapshots).

## 20. Baseline

A transparent four-signal scorecard (`src/models/baseline.py`), requiring no
training:

| Signal | Feature | Rule | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent revenue decline | 3-month revenue trend | <= -5% | 2.0 |
| Reduced transaction frequency | 3-month transaction trend | <= -5% | 2.0 |
| Increased inactivity | days since last activity | >= 30 | 1.5 |
| Weakening engagement | 3-month engagement trend | <= -5% | 1.5 |

Scores run 0-7; the binary flag uses score >= 3.0; the ranking value is score/7 and
is explicitly not calibrated. Missing values never add points. Ties break on worse
revenue trend, then transaction trend, then engagement trend, then `snapshot_id`.

Validation Precision@50 was 0.100 (lift 0.97); test 0.140 (lift 1.11). On this data
the decline-only scorecard is effectively random, which is a finding about the
generator, not a reason to dismiss rule baselines generally.

## 21. Model approach

- **Candidates:** logistic regression (standardised, one-hot segment) and histogram
  gradient boosting. Both inside a scikit-learn `Pipeline` with median imputation,
  most-frequent categorical imputation and `handle_unknown="ignore"` one-hot
  encoding, fitted on train only.
- **Selected:** histogram gradient boosting - validation Precision@50 0.380 vs 0.320
  for logistic regression, beyond the pre-registered 0.02 interpretability margin;
  PR-AUC effectively tied (0.274 vs 0.277).
- **Class imbalance:** 13.7% positive in train. No reweighting: a validation-only
  comparison showed no consistent ranking gain and a materially worse Brier score
  (0.086 to 0.114 for HGB). Imbalance is handled through the metric set.
- **No hyperparameter search.** Parameters were fixed before validation scoring.
- **No test-set tuning.** Selection, thresholds and calibration decisions used
  train/validation only; test was scored once after freezing the artifact.
- **No calibration layer.** Raw probabilities were already close (Brier 0.094).
- **Artifact:** `artifacts/model/revenuepulse_model.joblib` + `model_metadata.json`
  (feature list, label definition, periods, seed, parameters, selected metrics,
  synthetic flag, version).

## 22. Evaluation framework

Because the product prioritises rather than classifies, Precision@K is primary:

- **Ranking:** Precision@K, Recall@K, Lift@K, false-alarm rate at K
  (`FP / selected`), for K = 10, 25, 50 and top-10%/20% capacity.
- **Classification:** precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix at the configured 0.50
  threshold, PR-AUC; ROC-AUC as a secondary diagnostic.
- **Calibration:** Brier score plus a 10-bin equal-count table.
- **Uncertainty:** account-level percentile bootstrap (1,000 iterations, seed 42),
  resampling accounts to respect repeated snapshots.
- **Segments:** top-20% ranking within each segment, with reportability thresholds.
- **Errors:** high-scoring false positives, low-scoring false negatives, and the
  cases where the rule and the model disagree.
- **Not reported:** acceptance rate (no human experiment), ROI (no intervention
  data), real-world accuracy (no real data).

## 23. Actual synthetic results

Test split: 748 snapshot rows, 380 accounts, 94 positives, prevalence 12.6%.

| Model | P@10 | P@25 | P@50 | Recall@50 | Lift@50 | False alarm@50 | PR-AUC | ROC-AUC | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transparent scorecard | 0.200 | 0.200 | 0.140 | 0.074 | 1.11 | 0.860 | 0.136 | 0.540 | 0.243 |
| Logistic regression | 0.500 | 0.560 | 0.480 | 0.255 | 3.82 | 0.520 | 0.350 | 0.781 | 0.097 |
| Histogram gradient boosting | 0.600 | 0.720 | 0.540 | 0.287 | 4.30 | 0.460 | 0.382 | 0.756 | 0.094 |

Validation (778 rows, 80 positives): scorecard P@50 0.100 / PR-AUC 0.115; LR 0.320 /
0.277; HGB 0.380 / 0.274.

Account-level bootstrap: HGB P@50 0.540 [0.400, 0.700], PR-AUC 0.382 [0.292, 0.496],
difference vs scorecard +0.400 [+0.240, +0.560]. LR P@50 0.480 [0.320, 0.620].

Segments (HGB, top 20% within segment): Enterprise n=138, prevalence 0.159, P@20%
0.357, lift 2.24, PR-AUC 0.477; Mid-Market n=258, 0.105, 0.288, 2.76, 0.501; SMB
n=352, 0.128, 0.296, 2.31, 0.326. All reportable; none sufficient for a fairness
conclusion.

At the 0.50 threshold HGB flags 35 accounts: 23 true positives, 12 false positives
(precision 0.657, recall 0.245, F1 0.357).

Error analysis: 12 high-scoring false positives, of which 8 declined by less than
20% (median -6.4%); 15 low-scoring false negatives with maximum score 0.063 and
median realised change -24.6%; 9 accounts the scorecard caught that the model
missed; 15 the model caught that the scorecard missed.

**All of this is synthetic.** See `docs/Evaluation_Results.md` for full tables.

## 26. Pilot design

1. **Cohort:** one segment, 6-12 months of history, quarterly review cadence,
   150-400 accounts.
2. **Baseline:** run the client's current rule and RevenuePulse on the same
   quarter-ends, blind to each other, and compare at the same review capacity.
3. **Workflow:** weekly queue of 50 with mandatory human decision; notes on
   dismissals; exported log reviewed in the readout.
4. **Measurement:** triage time (before/after), acceptance/edit/dismiss distribution,
   Precision@K, false-alarm rate, reviewer-reported usefulness, at least one
   documented intervention.
5. **Governance:** privacy review before data transfer, deletion at day 30, named
   owner, rollback = stop using the queue.
6. **Exit:** readout with pre-agreed criteria; a "no" is a valid outcome.

## 28. Scaling

Only after a successful pilot: standardise onboarding to protect margin, add

## 29. Ethics and governance

- **Synthetic vs real boundary:** all current evidence is synthetic; no real-data
  claim is made.
- **Privacy:** aggregate-only fields; no names, emails, phones; pilot deletion after
  30 days; encryption in transit; minimum necessary data.
- **Prediction errors:** false positives waste review time; false negatives leave
  declining accounts unreviewed. Both are quantified and disclosed.
- **Biased prioritisation:** segment performance is reported but cannot establish
  fairness; real protected attributes are absent.
- **Automation bias:** explicit decision required; reason codes visible; override
  encouraged and logged.
- **Explainability:** rule-based reason codes plus model card; no LLM narrative.
- **Human override:** accept / edit / dismiss on every account.
- **Auditability:** local SQLite log with timestamp, score, AI suggestion, decision,
  edit and note.
- **Model drift:** not yet monitored; a pilot must track input drift, calibration and
  Precision@K per window.
- **Misuse:** prohibited uses are listed in the model card; no automated contact,
  pricing, offer or CRM action exists in the code, and a test asserts the product
  layer contains no outbound integration.
- **Rollback:** stop using the queue; the product writes nothing to source systems.

## 30. Launch blockers

STOP if any of the following is true:

1. No meaningful lift over the client's transparent rule on real data.
2. False-alarm rate is unacceptable to the reviewers who must use the queue.
3. Reviewer acceptance is poor or the queue is ignored.
4. Data access or onboarding makes the economics unworkable.
5. Privacy / governance controls cannot be established.
6. No measurable workflow benefit (triage time or decision quality).
7. Real-data model performance materially fails, including the growth-direction
   artefact not reproducing.

## 33. Limitations

1. Synthetic data only; no real-world performance established.
2. Proxy label (20% three-month decline), not churn.
3. Dominant learned driver (positive six-month revenue trend) is a generator
   property and may not transfer.
4. Weak rule baseline; the ML advantage is not yet measured against a strong simple
   rule.
5. Two test quarter-ends, 94 positives; wide intervals.
6. Recall@50 is 0.287: most decliners are outside the queue.
7. No calibration layer, no cost model, no threshold optimisation.
8. No human review, acceptance, time-saving or intervention evidence.
9. No authentication, monitoring, drift detection or production security.
10. Reason codes are descriptive, not exact per-account attributions.
11. Fairness cannot be assessed on synthetic segments.
12. Pilot pricing and economics are hypotheses; CAC is unknown.

## 34. Final recommendation

**ITERATE toward a controlled real-data paid pilot, subject to data, privacy and
governance validation. Do not scale; do not start production build-out.**

The synthetic evidence supports the *method*: leakage-safe features, validation-only
selection, capacity-aware evaluation, account-level uncertainty, and a ranking that
beats a transparent rule by a wide margin (P@50 0.540 vs 0.140; difference CI
[+0.240, +0.560]). It does not support any claim about real accounts.

Before the pilot: build a stronger simple baseline (level/volatility/trend) so the
comparison is honest, and confirm the growth-to-give-back relationship is not the
only signal doing the work. Then run the paid pilot with pre-agreed criteria, and
accept "stop" as a legitimate outcome.
