# Model Card - RevenuePulse AI revenue-decline risk model

**Version:** phase2-1.0.0
**Status:** Phase 2 research model. Not deployed. No UI, no automation.
**Data:** 100% synthetic. Every number below comes from
`python -m src.evaluation.evaluate` on the locked test split.

> **The model predicts a synthetic future revenue-decline proxy.
> It does NOT establish real-world churn prediction performance.**

## 1. Intended use

Rank B2B accounts by the estimated probability that revenue in the **next three
months** falls at least 20% below the trailing three-month average, so a sales or
account manager can spend a limited review budget on the highest-ranked accounts
first.

Intended use is **decision support for a human review queue**. The output is a
priority order and a probability estimate, plus descriptive reason codes. A human
decides what, if anything, to do next.

## 2. Intended users

- Sales managers and account managers responsible for a book of B2B accounts.
- Revenue operations analysts who maintain account data and review ranking
  quality.
- Faculty/reviewers assessing the capstone method.

Users are expected to understand that the score is an estimate with error, not a
fact about an account.

## 3. Prediction target

`future_revenue_decline` (binary). For a quarter-end snapshot `t`:

```text
reference = mean monthly revenue over the 3 months ending at t
future    = mean monthly revenue over the 3 months after t
label     = 1 if future <= reference * (1 - 20/100), else 0
```

Additional eligibility: at least 6 months of history and
`reference >= 5,000` synthetic currency units. The label is a **revenue-decline
proxy**, not confirmed churn or cancellation; the dataset has no cancellation
field. The label definition was fixed in Phase 1 and was **not** changed to
improve Phase 2 results.

## 4. Model type

Selected: **histogram gradient boosting classifier**
(`sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingClassifier`) inside a scikit-learn
`Pipeline`, preceded by a `ColumnTransformer`.

Candidates evaluated: transparent weighted scorecard (reference), logistic
regression, histogram gradient boosting. Selection used the validation split only
(see `docs/Evaluation_Results.md`, section 7).

## 5. Input features

Exactly the Phase 1 allow-list in `src/features/snapshots.py` (22 features):
`segment`, `snapshot_month`, `history_months`, the 3/6/12-month revenue means,
`revenue_trend_3m_pct`, `revenue_trend_6m_pct`, `revenue_volatility_6m`,
`transaction_mean_3m`, `transaction_trend_3m_pct`,
`revenue_per_transaction_3m`, `engagement_mean_3m`,
`engagement_trend_3m_pct`, `engagement_missing_ratio_3m`,
`days_since_activity_last`, `days_since_activity_mean_3m`,
`product_count_last`, `product_count_change_6m`,
`support_interactions_sum_3m`, `support_trend_3m_pct`, `contract_age_months`.

Excluded by construction: `account_id`, `snapshot_id`, `snapshot_date`, `split`,
`is_synthetic`, `label_eligible`, and every target-period column
(`reference_revenue_mean`, `future_revenue_mean`,
`future_revenue_change_pct`, `future_revenue_decline`,
`outcome_start_month`, `outcome_end_month`).

All features are computed from months on or before the snapshot date.

## 6. Data source

Generated by `src/data/generate_synthetic.py` from `config.yaml`
(400 accounts x 36 months, seed 42) and turned into quarter-end snapshots by
`src/features/build_snapshots.py`. No real company, customer, CRM, billing or
personal data is used anywhere in the repository.

**Synthetic-data disclaimer.** The generator embeds assumptions about trend,
seasonality, shock events, engagement and support behaviour. Model behaviour
reflects those assumptions, not market behaviour. Notably, in this generator a
positive six-month revenue trend is associated with a later 20% give-back, which
is the opposite of the usual churn intuition and is a property of the synthetic
data only.

## 7. Training approach

- One row = one account at one quarter-end snapshot.
- Fit on the **train** split only; preprocessing (median imputation, one-hot
  encoding, and standardisation for logistic regression) is fitted inside the
  pipeline on train rows only.
- No rows are dropped because of null short-history features
  (`revenue_trend_6m_pct`, `product_count_change_6m`, `support_trend_3m_pct`);
  they are imputed with training medians.
- Class imbalance (13.7% positive in train) is handled through the metric set
  (PR-AUC and Precision@K rather than accuracy), not reweighting. A
  validation-only check of `class_weight="balanced"` showed no consistent
  ranking gain and materially worse probability quality (see build notes).
- No hyperparameter search was run. Parameters are the small, documented set in
  `config.yaml`.

## 8. Temporal evaluation design

Time-ordered splits by snapshot date, never random:

| Split | Snapshots | Rows | Accounts | Positives | Prevalence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Train | 2023-06-30 to 2024-09-30 | 2,395 | 400 | 329 | 0.137 |
| Validation | 2024-12-31 to 2025-03-31 | 778 | 395 | 80 | 0.103 |
| Test | 2025-06-30 to 2025-09-30 | 748 | 380 | 94 | 0.126 |

Train fits, validation selects, test is scored once after the model is frozen.
The test split was not used for selection, threshold tuning or calibration.

## 9. Baseline comparison

Transparent scorecard: four binary signals (recent revenue decline, reduced
transaction frequency, increased inactivity, weakening engagement) with fixed
weights (2.0 / 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.5) and thresholds in `config.yaml`. It needs no
training.

On validation at the primary capacity of 50 reviews, the scorecard reached
Precision@50 = 0.100 (lift 0.97 versus 10.3% prevalence), i.e. no useful ranking
power. Logistic regression reached 0.320 and gradient boosting 0.380.

## 10. Performance

Locked test split, 748 snapshot rows, 380 accounts, 94 positives (12.6%):

| Model | P@10 | P@25 | P@50 | R@50 | Lift@50 | False-alarm@50 | PR-AUC | ROC-AUC | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transparent scorecard | 0.200 | 0.200 | 0.140 | 0.074 | 1.11 | 0.860 | 0.136 | 0.540 | 0.243 |
| Logistic regression | 0.500 | 0.560 | 0.480 | 0.255 | 3.82 | 0.520 | 0.350 | 0.781 | 0.097 |
| Histogram gradient boosting (selected) | 0.600 | 0.720 | 0.540 | 0.287 | 4.30 | 0.460 | 0.382 | 0.756 | 0.094 |

At the primary capacity of 50 reviews the selected model found 27 of 94 future
decliners (23 false positives among the 50 reviewed). Recall at that capacity is
0.287: **71% of future decliners are not in the top 50** and would be missed by a
review process limited to 50 accounts per quarter.

ROC-AUC is reported as a secondary diagnostic only; Precision@K, lift and PR-AUC
drive the read because the product is a prioritisation tool on imbalanced data.

## 11. Confidence intervals

Account-level percentile bootstrap, 1,000 iterations, seed 42, resampling the 380
unique test accounts and keeping each account's snapshots together:

| Metric | Point | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|
| Scorecard Precision@50 | 0.140 | 0.060 to 0.240 |
| Scorecard PR-AUC | 0.136 | 0.109 to 0.172 |
| Logistic regression Precision@50 | 0.480 | 0.320 to 0.620 |
| Logistic regression PR-AUC | 0.350 | 0.277 to 0.451 |
| HGB Precision@50 | 0.540 | 0.400 to 0.700 |
| HGB PR-AUC | 0.382 | 0.292 to 0.496 |
| HGB minus scorecard Precision@50 | +0.400 | +0.240 to +0.560 |
| HGB / scorecard Precision@50 | 3.86x | 2.15x to 9.33x |

The intervals are wide (a 94-positive test set), and the lower bound of
Precision@50 is 0.40. The advantage over the scorecard is positive across all
resampled account sets in this run, but that does not transfer to real data.

## 12. Known limitations

1. **Synthetic data.** No real-world churn or revenue performance is established.
   All separation may be an artefact of the generator.
2. **Label is a proxy.** It measures a 20% three-month revenue drop, not
   cancellation, and ignores recoveries, one-off billing and contract timing.
3. **Counter-intuitive dominant driver.** The fitted models rely most on
   `revenue_trend_6m_pct` with the *positive* direction (recent growth precedes
   give-back in this generator) and on low `engagement_mean_3m`. That direction
   may not hold in real accounts.
4. **Weak transparent baseline.** The decline-only scorecard is near random on
   this data, so the comparison flatters the learned model. A better baseline
   (for example a level/volatility rule) was not added, to avoid post-hoc tuning.
5. **Small test set.** 748 rows from 380 accounts, 94 positives; interval widths
   of ±0.14 Precision@50.
6. **Probability threshold is fixed at 0.50** from config. At that threshold
   recall is low (0.245 for HGB). Ranking, not the threshold, is the operational
   mechanism.
7. **Repeated snapshots.** 368 of 380 accounts appear in both test quarter-ends;
   bootstrap handles this, but the effective independent sample is 380 accounts,
   not 748 rows.
8. **No calibration layer.** Calibration is acceptable without it (Brier 0.094,
   mean absolute bin gap 0.030), and no calibration was fitted.
9. **Single seed, single split.** No rolling-origin evaluation across multiple
   quarters; stability over time is untested.
10. **Reason codes are descriptive rules**, not SHAP attributions; they do not
    decompose one account's score exactly.

## 13. Fairness and segment concerns

Synthetic segments only (`SMB`, `Mid-Market`, `Enterprise`). Test performance of
the selected model at 20% capacity within each segment:

| Segment | Rows | Prevalence | P@20% | Lift | PR-AUC | Threshold false-alarm rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | 138 | 0.159 | 0.357 | 2.24 | 0.477 | 0.462 |
| Mid-Market | 258 | 0.105 | 0.288 | 2.76 | 0.501 | 0.111 |
| SMB | 352 | 0.128 | 0.296 | 2.31 | 0.326 | 0.385 |

Every segment has enough rows to report, none has enough to conclude. Ranking
quality is weakest for SMB (PR-AUC 0.326, still above its 12.8% prevalence).
**Synthetic segment performance does not establish fairness.** Real attributes
(industry, region, contract size, tenure) are not in the data, so a real fairness
review is impossible at this stage. Segment-specific thresholds should not be
introduced from these numbers.

## 14. Explainability

- **Logistic regression (challenger):** standardised coefficients are exported to
  `artifacts/evaluation/logistic_coefficients.csv`. Largest magnitudes:
  `revenue_mean_12m` (-1.07), `revenue_mean_6m` (+0.93),
  `revenue_trend_6m_pct` (+0.64), `engagement_mean_3m` (-0.49),
  `revenue_per_transaction_3m` (-0.48). Positive coefficients raise the log-odds
  of the label.
- **Selected gradient boosting:** permutation importance on the validation split
  (`artifacts/evaluation/hgb_permutation_importance.csv`). Top features:
  `revenue_trend_6m_pct` (0.128), `engagement_mean_3m` (0.039),
  `revenue_trend_3m_pct` (0.009), `snapshot_month` (0.004),
  `days_since_activity_last` (0.002). Most other features, including
  `transaction_trend_3m_pct` and `engagement_trend_3m_pct`, have importance at or
  below zero on validation.
- **Account reason codes:** rule-based phrases evaluated on the account's own
  features, e.g. "recent revenue decline", "revenue decline over the last six
  months", "revenue well above its prior six-month average", "reduced transaction
  frequency", "increased inactivity", "weakening engagement", "low engagement
  level". Thresholds live under `reasons` in `config.yaml`.
  Coverage: 49 of the 50 highest-scoring test snapshots receive at least one code;
  40 of those 50 carry "revenue well above its prior six-month average", reflecting
  the synthetic driver.
- No LLM is used to generate explanations. Wording is always "factors associated
  with the risk score include...", never a causal claim.

## 15. Prohibited uses

- Automated customer contact, offers, discounting or pricing changes.
- Automated account termination, downgrade, collections or credit decisions.
- Any employment, individual-customer or personal-data decision.
- Presenting scores as churn facts, forecasts of individual account revenue, or
  evidence of real-world model performance.
- Using the model as the sole basis for a commercial decision without human
  review.
- Training on real customer data without a completed privacy, governance and data
  processing review.
- Claims that the model "predicts churn" or that it has been validated on real
  customers.

## 16. Monitoring requirements

Before any pilot use on real data:

- Track Precision@K, lift, PR-AUC and false-alarm rate per review window against a
  frozen baseline, with account-level bootstrap intervals.
- Monitor input drift in the top drivers (`revenue_trend_6m_pct`,
  `engagement_mean_3m`, activity recency) and the missing-rate of
  `engagement_score`.
- Monitor label prevalence and the realised outcome of reviewed accounts, and
  recheck the label definition against actual billing behaviour.
- Record human review decisions (accept/dismiss and reason) so that future
  evaluation can measure usefulness, not just ranking.
- Re-fit on a rolling window and re-run the validation comparison; never tune on
  the evaluation window.
- Watch segment-level ranking quality for degradation or systematic omission.

## 17. Conditions under which the model should not be used

- Without a human reviewer in the loop.
- On accounts with fewer than six months of history (excluded from training).
- For contract values below the 5,000 synthetic-unit revenue floor used by the
  label.
- For any account population materially different from the training distribution
  (different segments, geographies, product mix or billing cadence).
- When a 20% three-month revenue move would be driven by known one-off billing,
  seasonality or contract timing rather than account health.
- When probabilities are needed as calibrated business forecasts rather than
  ranking scores; the current output is validated for ordering only.
- When an audited real-world performance claim is required: none exists yet.
- In any regulated decision context without legal, privacy and governance review.
